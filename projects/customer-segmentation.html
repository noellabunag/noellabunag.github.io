Customer Segmentation & Energy Consumption Forecasting

This project explored how machine learning and statistical modelling can support customer segmentation and resource consumption forecasting.

Technologies used

  • Scikit-learn
  • Statsmodels
  • K-Means
  • ARIMA
  • SARIMA

01

Overview

This individual project had two parts. Part A used unsupervised learning to group customers into segments. Part B used time-series models to forecast electricity, gas and water consumption.

02

Customer Segmentation

Part A worked with a dataset of 200 customers. The aim was to find groups of customers with similar characteristics, so that each group could be understood and approached differently.

The workflow covered:

  • Exploratory data analysis
  • Data preprocessing
  • Feature scaling
  • K-Means clustering
  • Hierarchical clustering
  • Clustering evaluation

03

Exploratory Analysis

Before any modelling, I explored the customer data to understand how the features were distributed and how they related to one another. The features were then scaled so that no single feature dominated the distance calculations that clustering depends on.

Segment Insights

Among the five segments, the analysis highlighted high-income, low-spending customers as a potential target segment: customers who appear to have the capacity to spend more than they currently do.

The analysis estimated a 356% revenue growth opportunity for this segment under a hypothetical spending scenario. This is a modelled estimate based on assumptions — it is not revenue that was actually generated.

06

Energy Forecasting

Part B analysed consumption of three resources: electricity, gas and water. The work included:

  • Time-series analysis
  • ARIMA and SARIMA modelling
  • Model comparison
  • Hyperparameter tuning
  • Forecast evaluation

07

Model Evaluation

Models were compared on held-out test data. The best result was for electricity, where the forecast reached a test MAPE of 3.56% — on average, forecasts were within about 3.56% of the actual values on the test set.

08

Business Implications

  • Segmentation can help a business decide where to focus its marketing, such as on customers with unused spending capacity.
  • Forecasting can support planning for resource demand, with test error giving a realistic sense of how far to trust the forecast.

Both conclusions come from academic datasets and modelled scenarios, so they illustrate the approach rather than report results from a real business.

09

Key Learnings

  • Preprocessing matters for clustering. Scaling the features changes which customers end up grouped together.
  • Evaluate before you interpret. Checking cluster quality came before describing what each segment meant.
  • Seasonality needs the right model. Comparing ARIMA and SARIMA showed why it matters to test more than one approach on held-out data.
